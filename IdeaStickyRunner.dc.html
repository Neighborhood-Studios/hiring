We're hiring an
idea runner

Atlanta-based

An Idea Runner is the studio's point person on a new idea. When the team identifies a novel concept worth pursuing, the Runner picks it up and owns the next 4 to 8 weeks: a concentrated sprint of research, expert conversations, and direct customer discovery designed to answer one question: Is this idea as real as we think it is?

About the role

The role sits somewhere between researcher, product owner & mini-CEO. You'll build conviction — or kill it — through desk research, data analysis, expert calls & in-the-wild conversations with the people who would actually use, buy, or operate the product.

By the end of a sprint, you should have a clear, evidence-backed point of view on what the studio should do next: kill the idea, keep digging, or put it into the market. If the thesis holds up, you'll collaborate with the studio team — and, when relevant, an entrepreneur-in-residence — to design and launch a small-scale experiment. That often means running a version of the business by hand to see whether the idea holds up in practice.

The unique insight
"The best entrepreneurs know this: every great business is built around a secret that's hidden from the outside. A great company is a conspiracy to change the world; when you share your secret, the recipient becomes a fellow conspirator."
— Peter Thiel

A Runner's job is to find and pressure-test that secret. Every idea the studio pursues has a hypothesis at its core: something we believe to be true that the market hasn't fully recognized yet. Your job is to figure out whether we're right — and to be just as excited to discover that we're wrong.

What you'll do
  • Own an idea end-to-end. Pick up a freshly sourced concept and drive it through the studio's validation process — from initial screening through deep research, conviction building & when warranted, experimentation.
  • Get to the truth. Form hypotheses early, then try to break them. You're not building a case for an idea — you're building an honest picture of whether it holds up. Every important claim should be backed by data, a conversation, or direct observation.
  • Go deep, fast. Run 4-to-8-week research sprints that may include industry analysis, expert network calls, competitive and incumbent mapping, financial analysis, and direct outreach to potential customers and supply-side operators.
  • Talk to real people. Get out of the building. Cold-call franchisees. Walk into businesses. Interview operators. Pitch potential customers. A meaningful portion of your conviction should come from conversations that can't be Googled.
  • Surface what's non-obvious. Identify the unique insight at the heart of an idea, the thing the market may not see yet, and determine whether the evidence strengthens it, changes it, or dismantles it.
  • De-risk the idea. Identify the assumptions that have to be true for an idea to work, then systematically test them. Killing a bad idea early is just as valuable as validating a good one.
  • Help design and run experiments. Collaborate with the studio team and in some cases, the entrepreneur-in-residence, to scope and run low-fidelity experiments. Often, that means operating the business manually at a very small scale before building much of anything.
  • Wear many hats. In a single week, you might synthesize competitive intelligence, cold-email operators, build a financial model, vibe code a prototype, interview customers, or physically run a service. The work is varied by nature — that's the point.
  • Collaborate across the studio. Work alongside studio leadership, other Runners & entrepreneurs-in-residence. Share findings openly, pressure-test one another's logic & contribute to the studio's growing institutional knowledge.
What this role requires
  • Genuine curiosity that gives you energy. You’re the kind of person who gets a real charge from uncovering how an industry actually works, what's really driving a trend, or why a seemingly obvious idea hasn't been built yet. You’ll constantly jump into unfamiliar domains, and that should excite you rather than drain you.
  • An investigative mindset. You’re naturally inclined to pull on threads, question surface-level narratives, and dig until you understand what's actually going on. You treat assumptions as hypotheses to be tested, not facts to be confirmed.
  • Attention to detail. The difference between a good insight and a wrong conclusion often comes down to one data point, one conversation, or one assumption nobody bothered to check. You catch those things.
  • Comfort with ambiguity. You'll regularly start a sprint knowing almost nothing about an industry and be expected to develop a well-reasoned, evidence-backed point of view within weeks.
  • Bias toward action. You don't wait for permission to make the next call, send the next email, visit the next location, or test the next hypothesis. Velocity matters.
  • Willingness to do whatever the work demands. Sales calls one day, design mocks the next, vibe coding a prototype after that, and writing a research brief the morning after. You don't have a fixed lane, and you don't want one.
  • Strong communication skills. You can distill messy, ambiguous findings into a clear narrative, point of view, and recommendation.
  • Intellectual honesty. You change your mind when the evidence warrants it, and you're comfortable saying so clearly.
Nice to have

Prior experience as a founder, CEO, or early-stage operator. You know what it feels like to own something end-to-end, make decisions with incomplete information, and move quickly with limited resources.

From experiment to company

When an idea moves into experimentation, the Runner stays close to the work. You'll run experiments as a close partner to the entrepreneur-in-residence — or on occasion, run them entirely with the studio's direct support.

If an experiment becomes a launched company, the Runner transitions into a close advisor and thought partner to the founder of the spun-out venture. You won't disappear after the sprint — the knowledge you built during research and experimentation is too valuable for that. You'll stay connected as the company finds its footing.
